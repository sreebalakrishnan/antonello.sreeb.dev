1. What is your earliest memory of food?

My earliest memory is not really one particular dish. It’s more a feeling.

I remember the warmth of the fire, the smell of food just cooked, the soft colours around me, and that excitement of waiting for the food to be ready.

I think that feeling is still part of what pushes me today. It gives me energy to keep doing what I do. In the end, I don’t just want to give people food. I want to give them that feeling , the anticipation, the smell, the atmosphere, the pleasure of waiting for something good. An experience.

2. Why did you first come to India, and what made you stay?

The first time I came to India, honestly, it was just for a holiday.

But something made me come back. I don’t know exactly what. Maybe it was an ancestral call, maybe something spiritual, maybe just a feeling that there was something here for me.

I stayed because I decided to give myself a chance. A chance to learn, to share what I knew, to experiment and also to discover something new about myself.

And I think India was the right place to start.

3. Tell me about the first pasta you made here. What went wrong?

The first time I really put myself on the line, I created a recipe for whole-wheat tagliatelle.

For me, it was a disaster. 😂

I was looking at everything that was wrong with it. But the funny thing was that people were eating it and they were happy.

That made me understand something.

If people could already enjoy something that, in my eyes, was far from perfect, then I had a lot of space to improve my skills, experiment and create something that people could really appreciate.

That gave me motivation. I wanted to see how far I could take it.

5. What moment in your work has made you quietly proud?

I think I started to feel really proud when I realised that some of my best results happened when I wasn’t trying so hard to make something perfect.

Sometimes I would just cook. No big plan, no pressure. I would follow my instinct, make a recipe naturally, and the result would surprise even me. And then I would see how much people appreciated it.

That’s when I started understanding that maybe I really had something.

I also learned something about my ego.

Sometimes my ego comes in and I want everything to follow a precise idea in my head. I think too much, I create rules and expectations, and sometimes it simply doesn’t work.

But when I let that go and trust my instinct, the result can be completely different.

Sometimes, it’s sublime.

That’s probably when I understood that what I want to give people is not just a plate of food.

I want to give them an experience💪🏻💪🏻💪🏻
