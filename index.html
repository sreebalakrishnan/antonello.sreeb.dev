THE PERSON BEHIND THE PLATE

He came to India.
And found home.

The first time Antonello came to India, about 25 years ago, it was just a holiday. Pasta was almost impossible to find; he found one old packet in Pondicherry and joked that if he ever came back, he would make it himself. Something made him come back. “I stayed because I decided to give myself a chance. A chance to learn, to share what I knew, to experiment.”

The first recipe he put his name to here was a whole-wheat tagliatelle. “For me, it was a disaster. But people were eating it, and they were happy.” If people could enjoy something he thought was far from perfect, there was a lot of room to get better. He wanted to see how far he could take it.

For seven years that curiosity has taken him into hotel kitchens, new restaurant projects and celebrations. With Helena, it became Gastronómica Nutri, the pasta made slowly in Pondicherry.

“I don’t just want to give people food. I want to give them that feeling: the anticipation, the smell, the pleasure of waiting for something good.”— Anto

HELENA, ON ANTO

“The first time I took Antonello to a potluck, I explained: everybody cooks something at home, we meet, we share. He asked, ‘But do I need to cook for everybody?’ If he makes pizza, he brings four trays. There is this incredible sense of abundance in him, this need to make sure everyone is fed, and then fed a little more. And somehow, by the end of the evening, there's never a crumb left.”
