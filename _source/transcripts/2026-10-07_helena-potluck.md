The first time I took  Antonello to a potluck, I explained the concept to him: everybody cooks something at home, we all meet, and then we share everything.

He immediately asked, “But how many people will be there?”

“I don’t know,” I said. “It doesn’t really matter.”

He looked completely confused. “But do I need to cook for everybody?”

“No! You cook for the size of your family, and then everybody brings something and we share.”

I could see that he still didn’t quite understand.

And actually, after many potlucks, I’m not sure he ever did.

Because whenever Antonello brings food, he brings food for everybody. If he makes pizza, he doesn’t bring a few slices. He brings four trays of pizza.

There is this incredible sense of abundance in him, this need to make sure everyone is fed, and then fed a little more.

And every time I see the amount of food he has brought, I think, “Oh my…, if everybody brings this much, we’ll never finish all of this!”

But somehow, we always do.

So I’ve stopped trying to explain the concept of a potluck to Antonello. I just let him bring enough food to feed the entire neighbourhood and somehow, by the end of the evening, there’s never a crumb left.
